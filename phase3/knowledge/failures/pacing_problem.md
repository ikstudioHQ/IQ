# Failure Pattern: Undifferentiated Pacing (Long Flat Beats)

## What it looks like
A beat that's technically the right length but feels longer than it is
because nothing changes in rhythm, framing, or emotional intensity within
it — most likely in the "Conflict Introduction" beat, which at ~2 minutes
is by far the longest single beat in the 5-beat structure.

## Why it happens
`STORY.md` specifies beat *durations* (15 sec / 2 min / 1 min / 1 min /
30 sec) but not sub-beat rhythm within them. A 2-minute beat for a
2-5-year-old with no internal variation risks losing attention partway
through, since visual/rhythmic variety — not runtime alone — is the
primary retention driver at this age.

## How to avoid it
- Break the 2-minute conflict beat into 2-3 visually or emotionally
  distinct sub-moments internally (e.g. noticing the problem → reacting
  → trying something that doesn't quite work → the moment right before
  the character response beat), even though it's delivered as one
  continuous beat in the final script.
- Vary shot/scene framing within the beat rather than holding one
  composition for the full two minutes (see the production-intelligence
  / cinematography expansion work, when added, for concrete shot-type
  guidance).
- Cross-check against `rubric.md` dimension 9 (Child Engagement) —
  "a mid-episode pattern-interrupt keeps the 2-minute conflict beat from
  feeling like one long block" is scored there specifically because of
  this failure pattern.
