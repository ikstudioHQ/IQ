# SPONSORSHIP_POLICY.md — Screening Direct Sponsors & Brand Deals

Separate from AdSense/platform ad policy (which YouTube controls) — this
covers brands approaching the channel directly for sponsored segments,
product placement, or paid collaborations. One wrong sponsor here does
more damage to a trust-dependent religious-content channel than almost
any other content mistake, and it's not reversible after the fact the
way a bad episode is.

## Hard no-list (decline without negotiation)
- Interest-based (riba) financial products — loans, credit cards,
  "buy now pay later" services that function as interest-bearing credit.
- Alcohol, gambling, or anything alcohol/gambling-adjacent in branding.
- Any product or service that contradicts modest-dress or halal-food
  norms in its general marketing (even if the specific ad segment
  wouldn't show it) — brand association matters, not just the ad content.
- Anything targeting children with in-app purchases, loot-box-style
  mechanics, or manipulative "pester power" marketing design.
- Any sponsor unwilling to let the creator review the exact ad copy/
  claims before it airs.

## Requires actual due diligence (don't default-accept)
- Toy brands — check the specific product line for objectionable
  imagery/messaging, not just the brand's category.
- Educational apps/subscriptions — check what data they collect from
  child users and whether their own privacy practices would embarrass
  this channel if a parent looked into it.
- "Halal" or "Islamic"-labeled products — verify the certification claim
  actually holds up; a mislabeled product damages this channel's
  credibility by association even though the channel didn't make the
  claim.

## Fine, standard due diligence only
- General children's product categories with no specific red flags
  (books, non-toy educational products, plain apparel) — normal brand-fit
  and quality check, nothing Islamic-specific required.

## Disclosure
Any paid placement gets a clear, plain-language disclosure per platform
requirements (YouTube's paid promotion toggle at minimum) — this is a
legal requirement in most jurisdictions, not optional brand preference.
